# Salesforce to Zoho CRM Migration: A Practical Approach to Getting It Right.

Changing CRM platforms is a major decision for any business. Customer records, sales activities, reports, workflows, and integrations are often connected to everyday operations. Moving from Salesforce to Zoho CRM therefore requires more than exporting data and importing it into a new system. The real challenge is making sure the new CRM supports existing business requirements without carrying forward unnecessary complexity.

For organisations considering this transition, the best starting point is understanding what needs to change, what should remain, and how the migration will affect employees.

## Why Consider Moving From Salesforce to Zoho CRM?
Salesforce offers extensive customisation and functionality, but some businesses eventually find their CRM environment more complicated than their actual requirements.
As organisations grow, they may accumulate custom objects, automation rules, reports, integrations, and fields that require ongoing administration. In some cases, employees use only a small portion of the available features.
This can lead businesses to reconsider their CRM setup.

Common reasons include:

- Increasing subscription and maintenance costs.
- Difficulty managing complex configurations.
- Low adoption of available CRM features.
- Unnecessary customisations and workflows.
- Challenges maintaining connected applications.
- A need for a simpler approach to customer management.

However, switching platforms does not automatically resolve these issues. If the existing sales process is inefficient or customer data is poorly maintained, those problems can continue after migration.

## Prepare Before Moving Your CRM Data
A successful migration begins with an assessment of the existing Salesforce environment.

Start by identifying the information and functionality that employees depend on. This includes customer records, sales opportunities, activities, custom fields, reports, automation, and integrations.

### Review the Quality of Your Data
Check for duplicate contacts, outdated accounts, incomplete records, and inconsistent field values. Decide which information is still relevant and should be transferred.

Moving unnecessary data can make the new CRM harder to manage.

### Identify Essential Customisations
Review custom objects, Apex code, Visualforce pages, Salesforce Flows, and approval processes. These components may require redesign rather than direct migration.
For each customisation, determine whether it is still necessary, can be simplified, or should be retired.

### Understand Employee Requirements
Speak with sales representatives, managers, and administrators. Their daily experience can reveal important requirements that may not appear in technical documentation.

This assessment also helps establish the foundation for a professional Zoho audit and a more organised implementation plan.

## Understand the Difference Between Data and Functionality Migration
Not everything in Salesforce can be moved in the same way. Customer records, accounts, contacts, opportunities, notes, and activities may be transferred after appropriate preparation and mapping. Custom functionality requires a separate evaluation.

For example, Salesforce Apex code and Visualforce components cannot simply be copied into Zoho CRM. Workflows, reports, approval processes, and integrations may also need to be rebuilt.

A practical migration plan should classify each component as something to migrate, rebuild, simplify, or discontinue.

This prevents businesses from spending time recreating features that no longer support their operations.

## Follow a Structured Migration Process
A clear migration framework helps teams understand their responsibilities and reduces the likelihood of overlooking important dependencies.

### 1. Assess the Existing Salesforce Environment
Document the current data structure, customisations, integrations, automation, reports, and user permissions. Identify problems that should be addressed before the transition.

### 2. Design the Zoho CRM Structure
Decide how leads, contacts, accounts, opportunities, and sales activities should be organised. Avoid copying unnecessary complexity from the previous environment.

### 3. Map Fields and Records
Prepare a field mapping plan that connects Salesforce information with the appropriate Zoho CRM fields. Review data types, mandatory fields, picklist values, and record relationships.

### 4. Rebuild Important Automation
Identify which workflows and business rules are essential. Recreate them according to their purpose and test their behaviour before launch.

For specialised operational processes, businesses can also consider whether Zoho Creator has a role in business process automation. The decision should depend on actual requirements rather than adding functionality without a clear need.

### 5. Test the New Environment
Use a controlled testing environment before moving the complete dataset. Check record accuracy, permissions, automation, integrations, and reports.

Employees should also test everyday activities, such as creating leads, updating contacts, and moving opportunities through the sales process.

### 6. Plan the Final Transition
Establish when Salesforce data entry will stop and when Zoho CRM will become the primary system. Assign responsibilities for the final import, validation, communication, and issue resolution.

### 7. Monitor the System After Launch
Continue reviewing data quality, user adoption, and process performance after migration. Address issues before they become regular working habits.

## Why Post-Migration Monitoring Matters?
Many organisations focus heavily on completing the migration but pay less attention to the period immediately afterward.
The first 90 days can reveal problems that were not obvious during testing. Employees may struggle with unfamiliar workflows, managers may identify reporting gaps, and administrators may discover automation that needs adjustment.

Regular reviews help businesses understand whether the new CRM is working as intended.

A few areas deserve particular attention:

1. Are employees consistently updating customer records?
2. Are sales stages being followed correctly?
3. Do reports provide the information managers need?
4. Are integrations working as expected?
5. Are users experiencing difficulties with the new processes?

These questions address some of the issues that businesses often miss during the first 90 days of a Zoho implementation.

## Mistakes to Avoid During Migration
Even a carefully planned project can face difficulties when important details are overlooked.

**Transferring unnecessary information:** Old records and unused fields can reduce the quality of the new CRM.
**Recreating every Salesforce customisation:** Some functionality may be outdated or unnecessarily complicated.
**Ignoring integrations:** Connected applications may support essential business operations and must be checked before cutover.
**Skipping realistic testing:** Confirming that records have been imported is not enough. Business processes must also work correctly.
**Providing insufficient training:** Employees need guidance on the new CRM structure and their updated responsibilities.
**Neglecting post-launch reviews:** Migration is not complete from an operational perspective until the new system is stable and being used consistently.

## Consider the Total Cost of Ownership
Subscription pricing is only one part of the financial decision. Businesses should also account for data preparation, implementation, custom development, integration changes, testing, employee training, administration, and ongoing maintenance.

The existing Salesforce environment can significantly affect the amount of work required. A company using mostly standard functionality may have a different migration experience from one that depends on extensive custom development.

Understanding these costs before starting helps organisations make a more informed decision.

## Final Thoughts
Moving from Salesforce to Zoho CRM can give businesses an opportunity to simplify their CRM environment and improve how customer information is managed. However, the outcome depends on the quality of preparation. Reviewing existing processes, cleaning data, mapping fields carefully, rebuilding essential functionality, and involving employees throughout the transition all contribute to a more reliable result.

The objective should not be to recreate Salesforce inside Zoho CRM. It should be to build a practical system that supports the organisation's current requirements and remains manageable as the business develops.
